# Reference: Solvely.ai

Solvely is an AI study app (homework solver + study tools). It is Brofessor's **main product reference**: which features students like, and where the gap is that Brofessor fills.

Sources: Solvely App Store listing and user reviews (Oct 2026). Verify by using the app yourself.

---

## What Solvely does

**Inputs**
- Photo of a problem (textbook, worksheet, notebook, whiteboard)
- Imports: YouTube videos, PDFs, slides, web pages, recordings
- Lecture audio (transcribed)
- Follow-up questions to the AI tutor

**Outputs**
- Step-by-step solutions with reasoning for each step
- Lecture transcript, key takeaways, AI notes
- Quizzes, flashcards, podcast-style recap
- Algebra / statistics calculators, writing assistant
- Explanations adjusted to level (middle school to college)

**Subjects:** maths, physics, chemistry, biology, English, history, SAT/ACT/AP/GRE prep

**Pricing:** free download, about 2 free questions, then a subscription (~$12.99/month)

## What users like
- Shows *how* the answer is reached, not just the answer
- Fast photo-to-answer
- Notes, quizzes and tutor in one place

## User complaints
- Wrong answers in advanced topics (calculus, graphing, trigonometry)
- Paywall comes very early
- Weak support

## What Brofessor borrows
- [ ] **Photo input:** photo of a problem -> solved and taught on the board (Phase 5)
- [ ] **Multi-source import:** PDF and slides are planned; YouTube links and web pages are worth adding (extract transcript/text, then teach)
- [ ] **Level selector:** school / college / "from the very basics"
- [ ] **Follow-up doubts:** planned for Phase 6
- [ ] **Quiz + flashcards** at the end of a lecture
- [ ] **Recap:** Solvely does a podcast recap; Brofessor can do a "2-minute board recap" (fast replay of the board + summary)

## Where Brofessor is ahead
- Solvely has **no whiteboard teaching**. Explanations are text. This is Brofessor's core USP.
- No **voice synced with live drawing**
- No **Hinglish**
- Diagrams and graphs (where Solvely goes wrong) are drawn visually, and maths gets a verification pass (check answers with Python/SymPy)
